# BSM1 reproduction results

Two comparisons answer different questions. **How closely does SIMPO reproduce BSM1?** Compare SIMPO and MATLAB values for the same tank, substance and sample time within one case. **What does feedback control change?** Compare two SIMPO cases that receive the same weather inputs. Start with one dissolved oxygen reading in Tank5, then extend the comparison to the full time series and the whole plant.

The BSM1 report gives effluent and operating indices such as EQ and OCI. The NSE scores below were calculated for this SIMPO reproduction against trajectories from the supplied BSM1 Simulink models run in MATLAB R2024b. The MATLAB runs used `ode15s` for the two 500-day simulations and `ode45` for the six weather runs, with relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-8}$. The separate Assessment comparison gives the published benchmark values. [BSM1 report, appendices 4–5](https://www2.iea.lth.se/publications/Reports/LTH-IEA-7229.pdf#page=40) · [Assessment results and formulas](./Assessment.mdx)

### Why do the local runs for cases 03 and 04 score around 0.98 at 0.01% tolerance? {#dry-weather-wnse}

Of the 210 dry-weather curves, 15 track soluble inert organic matter (`S_I`), one in each of the five reactors and ten settler layers. All stay near **30 g COD/m³** throughout the 14 days. NSE divides the sum of squared prediction errors by the sum of squared deviations of the reference from its mean. Around 30 g COD/m³, even taking the mean can introduce a difference in the last digit stored by the computer. If the reference has only that much numerical variation, dividing by its tiny sum of squares magnifies roundoff. A tighter step tolerance refines the time integration. The stored concentrations still have the same numerical precision.

| 0.01% tolerance, 1,345 samples per curve | Locations with a roundoff-sized `S_I` difference | Maximum absolute `S_I` difference | Mean NSE of the other 195 curves |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 03 · Dry, open loop | Tank1 | 1.42 × 10⁻¹⁴ g COD/m³ | 0.99999586 |
| 04 · Dry, closed loop | Tank3, Tank4, and settler layers 6 and 10 | 3.55 × 10⁻¹⁵ g COD/m³ | 0.99999995 |

In case 03, Tank1’s `S_I` alone accounts for about **0.01379** of the gap to a perfect WNSE of 1. In case 04 the four `S_I` curves together cost about **4/210 = 0.01905** points, bringing WNSE to **0.98095**. The third column gives the corresponding concentration errors in physical units. Among the curves with a varying reference, the lowest NSE in 03 is **0.99990620** for `X_BH` in settler layer 3. In 04 it is **0.99999898** for DO in Tank5. The tolerance table above keeps all 210 curves at their original weights. <a href={useBaseUrl('/files/bsm1/NSE_by_series.csv')}>Errors by curve</a> · <a href={useBaseUrl('/files/bsm1/NSE_summary.csv')}>Full WNSE and group statistics</a>.

## Calculate NSE for a single curve {#score}

1. Match case, location, target, unit and timestamp. Each case compares **13 ASM1 components plus TSS**, at five reactors and ten settler layers, giving 210 curves in total. SIMPO counts layers upwards from the bottom. In the MATLAB export, layer 1 is at the top, so `Tank6_1` pairs with MATLAB layer 10. Under BSM1's settler model, reconstruct a layer's particulate concentration from its TSS and the particulate-to-TSS ratio in the current settler feed.
2. Use the MATLAB reference in the DataSet's **Measured** table and the simulation's **Predicted** trajectory. For cases 01–02 compare the entire approach to steady state, with constant influent and 3,001 samples taken every four hours from day 0 to day 500. For cases 03–08 compare 1,345 samples taken every 15 minutes from day 0 to day 14. To examine the dynamic assessment week, select the 673 samples from day 7 to day 14 inclusive.
3. For each curve, let $y_i$ be the MATLAB value, $p_i$ the SIMPO prediction and $\bar y$ the mean MATLAB value over the selected window. Use the formula below to calculate the Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency for that curve.

$$
\mathrm{NSE}=1-\frac{\sum_i (p_i-y_i)^2}{\sum_i (y_i-\bar y)^2}.
$$

**Try it with three actual samples.** In case 03, read three consecutive values of `Tank5.S_O2` starting at 168 h. The concentrations below are in g O₂/m³. The table rounds the readings for display and the calculation uses the original precision.

| Time (h) | Measured | Predicted | Prediction minus reference |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 168.00 | 0.4690170 | 0.4690096 | −0.0000074 |
| 168.25 | 0.4663545 | 0.4663674 | +0.0000129 |
| 168.50 | 0.4614744 | 0.4615023 | +0.0000279 |

The mean of the three reference readings is 0.4656153. Squaring and summing the differences in the last column gives a numerator of about $0.996\times10^{-9}$. Squaring each reference reading’s difference from the reference mean and summing gives a denominator of about $2.926\times10^{-5}$. The three-point NSE is **0.9999660**. Using all 1,345 samples gives **0.99999906** before rounding. The public report displays **1.0** for this Tank5 oxygen curve, including when the chart is zoomed to these 30 minutes.

**How do I interpret it?** With a varying reference, an unrounded NSE of 1 means the paired values agree exactly. Zero means the total squared prediction error equals the error of using the reference mean at every time. A negative value means the prediction has greater squared error. A single steady-state endpoint or a perfectly flat reference window has a zero denominator. Report the absolute difference $|p-y|$ in concentration units for a final value. To assess the entire approach to steady state, use the 3,001 samples spanning the run.

## Why case 07 is sensitive to the time step

At hour 262.75 of the storm run, the settler feed flow was about 78,363 m³/d in both models. Both also assigned almost the same conversion factor, **0.77875 g COD per g SS**, from layer TSS to active heterotrophic biomass (`X_BH`). For **layer 4 TSS**, MATLAB gave **862.50 g SS/m³**, while the saved 5% SIMPO run gave **632.42 g SS/m³**. Multiplying the 230.08 g SS/m³ gap by 0.77875 accounts for the **179.17 g COD/m³** gap in `X_BH`.

![Open-loop storm in settler layer 4. The 5% SIMPO run peaks one sample early. At 0.01% the points follow the MATLAB peak.](/img/bsm1/settler-storm-xbh.png)

The plotted samples are 15 minutes apart. The highest point during this event was at hour **262.25** in the saved run and **262.50** in MATLAB. The points at 262.25, 262.50 and 262.75 h contributed **96.81%** of this curve's full-run squared error. The settler limits downward solids flux below the feed by the smaller of the adjacent layers' settling fluxes. During a rapid change in flow, small differences in the layer concentrations before the peak can grow as solids move between layers.

Holding the input grid fixed and tightening the tolerance from 5% to 0.1% moved the sampled layer 4 peak to 262.50 h. Full-run `Tank6_4.X_BH` NSE rose from **0.970459** to **0.999865**. At 0.01% the largest error on this curve was **0.402 g COD/m³**. The top-layer effluent TSS already had NSE **0.999906** at 5%.

To locate this discrepancy in Dash, open <a href={projectLinks['07'] + '?result=simulation&tank=Tank6_4&target=X_BH'}>the case 07 layer 4 biomass chart</a> and zoom to 262–263 h. In the tank menu, `Tank6` combines the layers and `Tank6_4` selects only the fourth layer from the bottom. Switch the Target to **TSS**, then select **Tank6_10 → TSS** to check the top effluent layer at the same time. Holding the influent and tank parameters fixed while shortening the internal step brings the simulated peak closer to the MATLAB reference. Establishing the actual peak in a physical clarifier would require measured data from that layer.

## Check your own reproduction {#review}

| Where does the difference appear? | What to check next |
| --- | --- |
| The first sample already differs | Match loop mode, tank or layer, initial state and time units. Each weather case starts from its own conditioning run. |
| Peaks diverge when the influent changes | Check the weather input, Linear interpolation and Tank1’s first-order lag setting (8.64 s). Compare both trajectories at the same time. |
| Differences appear in closed loop | Pair true tank concentration, noisy sensor reading, PI command and effective handle with the corresponding MATLAB signals in turn. |
| A few settler layers differ in the storm | Keep the inputs fixed and change only Step Tolerance. Record the time, magnitude and layer of the largest difference. Check the top effluent layer separately. |
| Traces agree closely, but SP or OCI differs | Check the evaluation dates, units and final sludge inventory sample. [Compare the day 14 inventory endpoints](./Assessment.mdx#inventory) |

For your own case, record one concentration difference at a named time, one NSE with its location and window, and one Assessment value with its unit and evaluation dates. These three entries let another reader check a sample, the full curve and plant performance separately.
