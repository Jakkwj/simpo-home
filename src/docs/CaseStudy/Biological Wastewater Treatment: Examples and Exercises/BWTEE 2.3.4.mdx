### Aeration Calculation Method: BioModel

In the original case, the aeration calculation method was as follows:

    k_{L}a·(S_{O2,sat} - S_{O2})

  - $S_{O2,sat}: 0.28125 mmolO^{2}/l$
*   This reaction was then activated in the aeration tank within `Project.Activator`, thereby achieving the objective of continuously supplying oxygen to the system.

### Aeration Calculation Method: DataSet

The aforementioned calculation method is more suitable for simple laboratory simulations. In actual engineering, aeration calculations are typically performed within `DataSet`.


:::info Aeration Calculation Process in **SIMPO**


##### SOTR

*   Under standard conditions (i.e., 20°C and 1 atm), the density of air is approximately 1.204 kg/m³, with an oxygen mass fraction of about 23.15%. This means that 1 m³ of air contains approximately $1.204 * 0.2315 ≈ 0.279 kgO_2$.
*   The Standard Oxygen Transfer Rate (SOTR) is:

  $$
    SOTR (mgO2/d) = 0.279 (kgO_2/m³) * Qair (m³/d) * SOTE * 10^{6}
  $$
*   Where:
    *   $Qair$ is the dry-air flow rate referenced to 20°C and 1 atm, in m³/d
    *   SOTE is the Standard Oxygen Transfer Efficiency, used as a dimensionless fraction in the equations: 0.141 means 14.1%. Enter 14.1 in the `SOTE (%)` field of `Fit SOTE Curve`.

##### FOTR

*   Actual reactors typically contain wastewater at non-standard temperatures. Therefore, SOTR must be corrected to obtain the Field Oxygen Transfer Rate (FOTR):

  $$
    FOTR = dS_{O2}/dt * 1000 * V = SOTR * θ^{(T - 20)} * α * (β * S_{O2,sat} - S_{O2}) / S_{O2,sat,std}
  $$
*   Where:
    *   θ is the water-temperature correction factor, commonly taken as 1.024; its value should be consistent with the aeration equipment and test data.
    *   T is the water temperature, ℃
    *   $S_{O2,sat,std}$ is the saturated dissolved oxygen concentration under standard conditions (freshwater, 20°C, 1 atm). **SIMPO** uses the fixed table's 20°C value, approximately 9.09 mg/L; see [Edit a DataSet](../../Tutorials/DataSet/Edit.mdx#do-temperature) for the full precision and equation.
    *   $S_{O2,sat}$ is the saturated dissolved oxygen concentration in clean water at each Tank's water temperature. A Tank without a temperature series defaults to 20°C, using the same concentration as the standard reference and a temperature correction multiplier of 1.
    *   α is the Oxygen Transfer Ratio: $k_{L}a(Wastewater)/k_{L}a(Clean Water)$
    *   β is the Oxygen Solubility Ratio: $S_{O2,sat}(Wastewater)/S_{O2,sat}(Clean Water)$
    *   V is the reactor volume, m³

##### SOTE Curve Fitting

*   Blower manufacturers provide `SOTE-flow` curves under standard conditions, which also need to be corrected to obtain the Field Oxygen Transfer Efficiency (FOTE). In **SIMPO**, the following formula can be fitted to the standard `SOTE-flow` curve to determine a, b, and c. When b > 0, SOTE decreases as the air flow per diffuser increases. When 0\<b\<1 and other conditions remain fixed, the total oxygen transfer rate still increases with air flow, but at a diminishing rate.
  $$
    SOTE = a * (Q_d)^{-b} * H^c = a * (Qair / 24 / N)^{-b} * H^c
  $$
*   Where:
    *   $Q_d$ is the air flow rate per diffuser, m³/h/diffuser (please note that the time unit here is in **hours**. $Qair$ is in m³/d, so $Q_d=Qair/24/N$. Internally, Flow is in m³/s, giving the equivalent conversion $Q_d=3600Qair_s/N$)
    *   N is the number of diffusers (`Diffuser Number`)
    *   H is the diffuser installation depth, m

##### Water-Temperature Correction of Saturated Dissolved Oxygen {#do-temperature}

For the water-temperature correction formula, temperature input, and sampling rules, see [Edit a DataSet: Water-Temperature Correction of Saturated Dissolved Oxygen](../../Tutorials/DataSet/Edit.mdx#do-temperature).

##### Solving the Equation

*   Combining the above three equations yields the following, with $279/S_{O2,sat,std}\approx30.68488$ and full precision used in calculations:
    $$
      dS_{O2}/dt = 30.68488 * Qair * a * (Qair / 24 / N)^{-b} * H^c * θ^{(T - 20)} * α * (β * S_{O2,sat} - S_{O2}) / V
    $$
*   If the aeration equation uses $k_{L}a * (S_{O2,sat} - S_{O2})$, as in this case, BSM1, and BSM2, this corresponds to β = 1. Then:
    $$
      k_{L}a = 30.68488 * Qair * a * (Qair / 24 / N)^{-b} * H^c * θ^{(T - 20)} * α / V
    $$
*   If β = 1, α = 1, a = 1, b = 0, and c = 0, this simplifies further to the <b>conversion relationship between $k_{L}a$ and $Qair$</b>:
    $$
      k_{L}a = θ^{(T - 20)} * 30.68488 / V * Qair
    $$
*   In these equations, $dS_{O2}/dt$ is in **$mgO_2/L/d$** and $k_{L}a$ is in $d^{-1}$. Divide $k_{L}a$ by 24 to express it in $h^{-1}$.



:::


This case targets $k_{L}a=100 h^{-1}=2400 d^{-1}$ at the actual water temperature, including the α and θ corrections. The required air flow is calculated using the current parameters:

*   N (`Tank` parameter `Diffuser Number`): 1
*   H (`Tank` parameter `Diffuser Depth`): 0.5 m
*   α (`Tank` parameter `Alpha Factor`): 0.55
*   β (`Tank` parameter `Beta Factor`): 1
*   a (`Pump` parameter `SOTEa`): 0.3259
*   b (`Pump` parameter `SOTEb`): 0.3142
*   c (`Pump` parameter `SOTEc`): 0.2567
*   θ (`Pump` temperature correction factor): 1.024
*   V: 0.1 m³
*   $S_{O2,sat,std}$: 9.092426042885567 mg/L
*   T (the Tank's temperature in `Environment`): 20.51°C, giving $\theta^{T-20}=1.024^{0.51}\approx1.01216887$

Substituting into the [air-flow inversion equation in Edit a DataSet](../../Tutorials/DataSet/Edit.mdx#airflow-from-kla) gives:
$$
Qair=\left[\frac{2400\times9.092426042885567\times0.1}
{279\times0.55\times0.3259\times0.5^{0.2567}\times1.024^{20.51-20}\times(24\times1)^{0.3142}}\right]^{1/(1-0.3142)}
\approx73.08141768\;m^3/d
$$

Set the air flow in `DataSet.Flow` to **73.0814 m³/d**, referenced to dry air at 20°C and 1 atm.

The corresponding air flow per diffuser is $Q_d=Qair/(24N)\approx3.04506$ m³/h/diffuser, with an SOTE of approximately 0.192249 (19.2249%). Substituting the air flow back into the aeration equation:
$$
 k_{L}a = \frac{279\times73.0814\times0.3259\times(73.0814/24/1)^{-0.3142}\times0.5^{0.2567}\times1.024^{20.51-20}\times0.55}{9.092426042885567\times0.1}
 \approx2400\;d^{-1}=100\;h^{-1}
$$

This air flow follows the stated SOTE curve; confirm that it falls within the curve's validated flow range before applying it. Aeration is supplied by `DataSet.Pump` here, so disable the `BioModel` aeration reaction in this Tank to avoid counting it twice.

This case expresses oxygen concentration in mmol O₂/L. Divide concentrations in mg O₂/L by 32; do not divide $k_{L}a$ by 32:
$$
\frac{dC_{mmol}}{dt}=k_{L}a\left(\frac{S_{O2,sat}}{32}-C_{mmol}\right)
$$
Using the [water-temperature correction of saturated dissolved oxygen](../../Tutorials/DataSet/Edit.mdx#do-temperature), $S_{O2,sat}\approx9.00115704$ mg O₂/L at 20.51°C, or approximately 0.28128616 mmol O₂/L. Concentration and its rate of change are converted together, leaving $k_{L}a$, whose unit is inverse time, unchanged.

### Summary

In practical use, different methods for aeration calculation can be selected based on the actual situation:

**1. For simple laboratory simulations:**

*   Use default parameters for `DataSet.Tank` (i.e., N=0, H=0, α=1, β=1).
*   Add an 'aeration reaction' line in `BioModel`: $k_{L}a·(S_{O2,sat} - S_{O2})$.
*   Activate this reaction in the aeration tank within `Project.Activator`.

**2. For more precise simulations in engineering projects:**

*   Directly fit the parameters a, b, and c in **SIMPO** based on the manufacturer's `SOTE-flow` standard condition curve.
*   Connect `DataSet.Pump` to `DataSet.Tank` and set the air inflow rate `Qair`.
*   Set the `DataSet.Tank` parameters (N, H, α, β), θ in `DataSet.Pump`, and T in `DataSet.Environment` according to actual conditions.
